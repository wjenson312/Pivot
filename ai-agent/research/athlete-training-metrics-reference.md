# Athlete Training Metrics Reference

A comprehensive catalog of metrics athletes and coaches use to guide training, spanning general physiology through sport-specific and advanced clinical measures. Organized from simple/cheap to complex/expensive within each category.

---

## 1. Body Composition & Anthropometrics

- **Body weight** — Daily or weekly mass tracking used to monitor fueling, weight-class targets, or unintended loss/gain.
- **Height / limb length** — Fixed anthropometric baseline used to normalize other metrics (e.g., stride length, wingspan-to-height ratio).
- **Body fat percentage** — Proportion of mass that is fat vs. lean tissue, used to track physique changes relative to performance.
- **Lean body mass (fat-free mass)** — The non-fat component of body weight, tracked to ensure training/nutrition is preserving or building muscle.
- **Waist-to-hip ratio** — A simple tape-measure ratio used as a proxy for central adiposity and general health risk.
- **DEXA scan (bone density + regional body composition)** — X-ray-based imaging that precisely maps fat, lean mass, and bone mineral density by body region.
- **Bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA)** — Sends a small electrical current through the body to estimate fat/water/lean mass from resistance.
- **Skinfold measurements** — Calipers pinch subcutaneous fat at standard sites to estimate body fat percentage cheaply.
- **Muscle cross-sectional area (ultrasound/MRI)** — Imaging-based measurement of individual muscle size, used in rehab and hypertrophy tracking.
- **Somatotype** — Classification of body build (ectomorph/mesomorph/endomorph) used for talent identification and sport suitability.

## 2. Strength & Power

- **1-rep max (1RM)** — The heaviest load an athlete can lift once for a given lift, the classic strength benchmark.
- **Relative strength (1RM / bodyweight)** — Strength normalized to body mass, important for sports where athletes move their own weight.
- **Vertical jump height** — Countermovement or squat jump height, a fast proxy for lower-body power.
- **Broad jump distance** — Horizontal jump distance, used to assess explosive horizontal power.
- **Rate of force development (RFD)** — How quickly force is generated from the start of a muscle contraction, critical for sports needing fast, forceful movements.
- **Peak power output (watts)** — The maximum instantaneous power generated during a jump, sprint, or lift, often measured via force plate or power meter.
- **Isometric mid-thigh pull (IMTP) peak force** — A standardized strength test measuring maximal force production without joint movement, widely used because it's low-injury-risk.
- **Grip strength** — Handheld dynamometer measurement correlated with general strength and, in some studies, overall health/longevity.
- **Force-velocity profile** — A curve mapping how much force an athlete produces across a range of movement speeds, used to identify whether they need force- or velocity-focused training.
- **Reactive strength index (RSI)** — Jump height divided by ground contact time, quantifying an athlete's ability to use the stretch-shortening cycle (elasticity).
- **Asymmetry index (left/right force or power)** — Percentage difference in force output between limbs, used to flag injury risk or incomplete rehab.
- **Eccentric strength (e.g., Nordic hamstring force)** — Force produced while a muscle lengthens under load, a key predictor of hamstring/soft-tissue injury risk.

## 3. Speed, Acceleration & Agility

- **Top speed (max velocity)** — The highest sprint speed an athlete reaches, typically measured with radar, GPS, or timing gates.
- **Acceleration (0-10m, 0-20m splits)** — Time to cover short distances from a standing start, reflecting first-step quickness.
- **Flying sprint time** — Time over a set distance with a running start, isolating maximum speed from acceleration ability.
- **Time to peak velocity** — How many meters/seconds it takes an athlete to reach their top speed, useful for distinguishing accelerators from top-end sprinters.
- **Deceleration capacity** — Rate at which an athlete can safely slow down, increasingly tracked because high deceleration loads correlate with injury.
- **Agility test times (e.g., 5-10-5 pro agility, T-test)** — Timed change-of-direction drills measuring the ability to decelerate, redirect, and re-accelerate.
- **Reactive agility** — Change-of-direction speed in response to an external stimulus (light, opponent movement) rather than a pre-planned pattern.
- **Stride length and stride frequency** — The distance covered per step and the number of steps per second, whose combination determines running speed.
- **Ground contact time** — How long the foot stays on the ground during a stride, with shorter times generally indicating more efficient, elastic running.

## 4. Endurance & Cardiovascular Metrics

- **Resting heart rate** — Beats per minute at rest, where a lower value often reflects better cardiovascular fitness or recovery status.
- **Maximum heart rate** — The highest heart rate an athlete can reach under exertion, used to set training zones.
- **Heart rate zones (Z1-Z5)** — Percentage bands of max heart rate used to prescribe and monitor training intensity.
- **VO2 max** — The maximum rate of oxygen the body can utilize during exercise, considered the gold-standard marker of aerobic capacity.
- **Lactate threshold** — The exercise intensity at which lactate begins accumulating in the blood faster than it can be cleared, marking the upper limit of sustainable aerobic effort.
- **Functional threshold power (FTP)** — In cycling, the highest power an athlete can sustain for about an hour, used to set all training zones.
- **Critical velocity / critical power** — The theoretical highest pace/power sustainable indefinitely before fatigue accelerates, derived from time-trial modeling.
- **Heart rate variability (HRV)** — Variation in time between heartbeats, widely used as a daily readiness and autonomic recovery indicator.
- **Heart rate recovery** — How quickly heart rate drops after stopping exercise, a marker of cardiovascular fitness and autonomic function.
- **Running/cycling economy** — The amount of oxygen or energy required to sustain a given speed or power, reflecting efficiency independent of raw aerobic capacity.
- **Blood lactate concentration** — Direct blood sample measurement (via lactate meter) during graded exercise to pinpoint training zones precisely.
- **Respiratory exchange ratio (RER)** — Ratio of CO2 produced to O2 consumed, used in metabolic testing to determine whether the body is burning more fat or carbohydrate.

## 5. Biomechanics & Movement Quality

- **Joint range of motion (ROM)** — Goniometer or sensor-based measurement of how far a joint can move, used to screen mobility deficits.
- **Knee/hip/shoulder rotation angles during activity** — Sport-specific joint angle tracking (e.g., via IMU) used to assess technique and injury risk.
- **Ground reaction force (GRF)** — The force exerted by the ground back on the body during contact, measured via force plates to assess loading patterns.
- **Landing mechanics (knee valgus, trunk lean)** — Assessment of how the body absorbs force on landing, a major screen for ACL injury risk.
- **Joint loading rate** — How quickly force accumulates at a joint during impact, linked to overuse injury risk in running and jumping sports.
- **Movement asymmetry (kinematic)** — Differences in joint angles or timing between left and right sides during a movement, used to detect compensation patterns.
- **Center of mass displacement** — Tracking of the body's balance point during movement, used in gait and stability analysis.
- **Stride/gait symmetry** — Comparison of left vs. right step timing and force in walking/running, used in return-to-play and injury screening.
- **Angular velocity of a joint or limb segment** — How fast a body part rotates during a movement (e.g., pelvis or shoulder in a throw/swing), captured via IMU or motion capture.
- **3D motion capture kinematics** — Full-body marker-based tracking of joint angles and segment motion in a lab, the gold standard for detailed technique analysis.
- **Muscle activation timing/sequencing (EMG)** — Electromyography measurement of which muscles fire and in what order during a movement, used for technique and rehab diagnostics.
- **Postural sway / balance score** — Measurement of body wobble on a stable or unstable surface, used to assess proprioception and concussion/ankle-injury recovery.

## 6. Training Load & Injury Risk Management

- **Session RPE (rating of perceived exertion)** — A simple 1-10 self-reported effort score multiplied by session duration to quantify training load.
- **Total distance covered (GPS)** — Distance run in a training session or game, a foundational external load metric in field sports.
- **High-speed running distance** — Distance covered above a set speed threshold, used to quantify the most demanding running load.
- **Sprint count** — Number of efforts above a maximal-speed threshold in a session, tracked because sprint volume drives certain soft-tissue injuries.
- **Player load / acceleration load (accelerometer-derived)** — A composite score from wearable accelerometers summarizing total physical stress across all movement directions.
- **Number of accelerations/decelerations** — Count of high-intensity speed changes, which load the body differently than steady running.
- **Acute:chronic workload ratio (ACWR)** — Ratio of recent (acute) training load to longer-term (chronic) average load, used to flag spikes associated with injury risk.
- **Training monotony and strain** — Statistical measures of day-to-day training variability, where low variability (monotony) combined with high load is linked to overtraining/illness risk.
- **Total impacts/collisions** — Count and severity of hits or landings (from wearables), especially relevant in contact sports and concussion monitoring.
- **Injury history / days lost to injury** — Longitudinal record of past injuries used to contextualize current risk and load decisions.
- **Return-to-play readiness score** — A composite of strength symmetry, ROM, and functional tests used to clear an athlete after injury.

---

## 7. Recovery & Readiness

- **Sleep duration** — Total hours slept per night, foundational to recovery, hormone regulation, and reaction time.
- **Sleep quality/efficiency** — Percentage of time in bed actually spent asleep, and time spent in deep/REM stages, tracked via wearables.
- **Subjective wellness questionnaire (soreness, mood, stress, fatigue)** — Simple daily self-report scores used to flag athletes trending toward overreaching.
- **Resting HRV trend** — Day-over-day change in heart rate variability used as an early warning sign of accumulated fatigue or illness.
- **Perceived recovery scale** — A quick self-rated score of how recovered an athlete feels before a session.
- **Cortisol:testosterone ratio** — A blood/saliva hormone ratio used to assess whether an athlete is in a catabolic (breakdown) or anabolic (building) state.
- **Creatine kinase (CK) levels** — A blood biomarker of muscle damage, elevated after intense eccentric or high-volume training.
- **Body temperature trends** — Basal or skin temperature tracking used to detect illness or overtraining before symptoms appear.
- **Menstrual cycle tracking** — Monitoring cycle phase to adjust training load and expectations around hormonal fluctuations in female athletes.
- **Muscle soreness (DOMS) rating** — Self-reported delayed-onset muscle soreness score used to guide next-session intensity.
- **Neuromuscular readiness (jump test deviation)** — Day-to-day change in a quick jump test (e.g., CMJ height) used as an objective proxy for nervous system fatigue.

---

## 8. Blood, Hormonal & Advanced Biochemical Markers

- **Complete blood count (CBC)** — Standard panel of red/white blood cells and platelets used to screen for anemia, immune status, and overtraining.
- **Ferritin / iron studies** — Blood iron storage levels, critical in endurance athletes because low iron impairs oxygen transport and performance.
- **Vitamin D level** — Blood marker linked to bone health, immune function, and muscle performance, commonly deficient in indoor/winter athletes.
- **Testosterone (total/free)** — Hormone level used to assess anabolic status, recovery capacity, and (in some contexts) overtraining syndrome.
- **Cortisol** — The primary stress hormone, whose chronic elevation can signal overtraining or inadequate recovery.
- **Thyroid panel (TSH, T3, T4)** — Hormone markers used to rule out metabolic issues that could explain unexplained fatigue or weight changes.
- **hs-CRP (C-reactive protein)** — A blood marker of systemic inflammation, used to monitor recovery from illness or excessive training stress.
- **Blood glucose / HbA1c** — Markers of blood sugar regulation, relevant to fueling strategy and metabolic health.
- **Lipid panel (cholesterol, triglycerides)** — Cardiovascular health markers tracked as part of general athlete health monitoring.
- **Omega-3 index** — Blood marker of anti-inflammatory fatty acid status, increasingly used to guide supplementation for recovery.
- **Genetic testing (e.g., ACTN3, ACE genotype)** — DNA markers loosely associated with power vs. endurance predisposition, used in some talent-ID programs (limited predictive power).
- **VO2 kinetics / metabolic cart testing** — Lab-based breath-by-breath gas analysis providing detailed aerobic/anaerobic energy system contributions.

---

## 9. Cognitive, Neurological & Reaction Metrics

- **Simple reaction time** — Time to respond to a single known stimulus, a base measure of nervous system processing speed.
- **Choice/complex reaction time** — Time to respond correctly when multiple possible stimuli/responses exist, more sport-relevant than simple reaction time.
- **Visual tracking/perception scores** — Tests of how well an athlete tracks moving objects, relevant to ball sports and sports vision training.
- **Decision-making accuracy under fatigue** — Change in correct-decision rate as physical fatigue increases, used in team-sport cognitive testing.
- **Baseline/post-concussion cognitive testing (e.g., ImPACT)** — Standardized computerized tests comparing cognitive function before and after a suspected head injury.
- **Working memory / attention span tests** — Cognitive assessments sometimes used in sports requiring rapid tactical processing.

---

## 10. Nutrition & Hydration Metrics

- **Daily caloric intake** — Total energy consumed, tracked to match fueling with training demands.
- **Macronutrient breakdown (protein/carb/fat)** — Proportion of intake from each macronutrient, adjusted based on training phase and goals.
- **Hydration status (urine specific gravity or color)** — A simple test estimating fluid balance, since even mild dehydration impairs performance.
- **Sweat rate / sweat sodium concentration** — Measurement of fluid and electrolyte loss during exercise, used to build individualized hydration plans.
- **Energy availability (EA)** — Dietary energy remaining after exercise expenditure relative to lean mass, critical for detecting Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S).
- **Glycogen depletion status** — Indirect or biopsy-based estimate of stored carbohydrate fuel, relevant to endurance pacing and fueling strategy.

## 11. Sport-Specific Metrics

### Running / Track & Field
- **Split times per lap/interval** — Pacing consistency across a race, used to evaluate race strategy execution.
- **Cadence (steps per minute)** — Running step rate, linked to injury risk and efficiency when too low.
- **Vertical oscillation** — How much the body bounces up and down per stride, where excess movement wastes energy.
- **Throwing/jumping event distances (shot put, javelin, long jump, etc.)** — The direct competitive result, tracked across a season for progression.

### Team/Field Sports (Soccer, Football, Rugby, Basketball, Lacrosse, Hockey)
- **Distance covered per position/game** — Total running volume by role, used to benchmark positional demands.
- **Number of sprints per game** — Count of maximal efforts, reflecting the repeated-sprint nature of the sport.
- **Time-motion analysis (walking/jogging/sprinting %)** — Breakdown of a game into intensity categories to characterize its physical demands.
- **Tackles/hits/collisions count and force** — Contact load metrics, especially important in football/rugby for head and body injury monitoring.
- **Shooting/passing accuracy** — Technical skill success rate, tracked to connect physical conditioning with on-field output.
- **Vertical leap for rebounding/blocking (basketball/volleyball)** — Sport-specific application of vertical jump ability.

### Swimming
- **Stroke rate and stroke length** — Number of arm cycles per minute and distance covered per cycle, whose product determines swim speed.
- **Turn/wall time** — Time spent on flip turns and push-offs, a major differentiator in short-course racing.
- **Distance per stroke (DPS)** — Efficiency metric showing how far the swimmer travels per stroke cycle.
- **Stroke index** — A composite of swim velocity and stroke length used to compare technical efficiency across swimmers.

### Cycling
- **Power output (watts) and normalized power** — Real-time and load-adjusted power measured via a power meter, the primary training metric in cycling.
- **Power-to-weight ratio (w/kg)** — Power output normalized to body mass, the key determinant of climbing performance.
- **Cadence (RPM)** — Pedaling rate, optimized differently for sprinting vs. endurance riding.
- **Training stress score (TSS)** — A composite score combining intensity and duration of a ride relative to FTP, used to quantify overall training load.

### Combat Sports (Boxing, MMA, Wrestling, Judo)
- **Punch/strike force and speed** — Force plate or sensor-based measurement of striking power and hand speed.
- **Grip endurance** — Time-to-fatigue in a sustained grip test, critical in wrestling/judo/BJJ.
- **Weight-cut tracking** — Monitoring of hydration and body mass in the lead-up to weigh-ins for weight-class sports.
- **Takedown/clinch success rate** — Technical statistic tracking effectiveness of core technique categories.

### Throwing/Racquet Sports (Baseball, Tennis, Golf, Javelin)
- **Throwing/pitch velocity** — Radar-measured speed of a throw or pitch, a primary performance benchmark.
- **Arm/joint torque during throwing** — Biomechanical stress on the elbow/shoulder during a throw, used heavily in pitcher injury prevention (e.g., UCL risk).
- **Bat/racquet/club head speed** — Speed of the implement at the point of contact, directly tied to power output in the swing.
- **Spin rate** — Rotational speed of a thrown or struck ball (baseball pitches, golf shots), affecting trajectory and movement.
- **Exit velocity / ball speed off contact** — Speed of the ball immediately after being struck, a key power metric in baseball, golf, and tennis.

### Weightlifting / CrossFit / Strength Sports
- **Total lifted across competition lifts** — Combined competition result (e.g., snatch + clean & jerk), the direct competitive outcome.
- **Bar velocity (m/s) via velocity-based training** — Speed of the barbell during a lift, used to autoregulate training load in real time.
- **Wilks/DOTS score** — Formulas normalizing lifted weight against bodyweight to compare strength across weight classes.

### Jumping/Court Sports (Volleyball, High Jump)
- **Approach jump height** — Vertical jump measured with a running approach, more sport-specific than a standing vertical.
- **Block/attack touch height** — Maximum reach height achieved during a jumping action at the net.

### Rowing
- **Split time (per 500m)** — The core pacing metric in rowing, analogous to pace in running.
- **Stroke rate (strokes per minute)** — Rowing cadence, balanced against power per stroke to optimize speed.
- **Force curve per stroke** — Shape of force application throughout the rowing stroke, used to refine technique on an ergometer.

### Gymnastics/Diving
- **Execution score / difficulty score** — Judged scoring components used to track technical proficiency and routine difficulty over time.
- **Air time / rotation speed** — Time spent airborne and rotational velocity during aerial skills, tracked to refine timing and technique.
